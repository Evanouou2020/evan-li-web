Earthquake Monitoring

Outside of this site, I run my own real-time earthquake detection and early-warning system out of my house — watching live seismic data, catching small local quakes before they hit the news, and pushing alerts to Discord and phones within seconds of the ground moving.

How it works

  • Streams live seismic waveform data over the SeedLink protocol from IRIS/EarthScope's real-time feed, across six hand-picked Northern California stations chosen because they're actually available in real time.
  • Every station runs classic STA/LTA (short-term vs. long-term average) trigger detection, cross-checked against a machine-learning phase-picking model (PhaseNet) so a single noisy station can't false-trigger on its own.
  • A real event requires at least 3 stations to agree within a 15-second window before anything gets called — a "coincidence trigger."
  • A full SeisComP pipeline runs in parallel for official-grade location and magnitude solving, pulling in extra regional networks through a custom bridge for stations that aren't on the public real-time feed.
  • For nearby events, it estimates P-wave and S-wave arrival time to home and can fire an early-warning-style countdown before the shaking arrives.

Alerts

  • Discord — rich alert embeds with waveform and map screenshots attached, magnitude-tiered colors, an automatic "Drop, Cover, and Hold On" safety card for larger events, and an @everyone ping above a set magnitude threshold.
  • ntfy — simple push notifications straight to a phone, no account required.
  • Email — optional, for full event reports.

Live station feed — PB.B054, Berkeley Hills (Sibley)

One of the stations I stream from is PB.B054, a Plate Boundary Observatory borehole station up in the Berkeley Hills near Sibley — about 25 km from here. This is the same live waveform viewer from my own dashboard, embedded directly. Use the zoom bar at the top of the panel (10 seconds up to 2 hours) to zoom in and out on the signal.

Other tools

  • Auto-generated focal mechanism (beachball) plots from first-motion picks.
  • Regional and global station maps.
  • A false-trigger checker that cross-references live flight-tracking data — low-flying planes can sometimes look like a tiny quake to a sensitive station.
  • SeismoPhone — a companion tool that turns a phone's accelerometer into a makeshift seismometer.
